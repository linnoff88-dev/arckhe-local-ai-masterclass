Sovereignty, independence, your own foundation.

🏠 What local AI really is

Where the model runs, what an LLM is, and the three pillars in practice.

What it is:

In the cloud, the prompt travels to a data center; locally, everything happens on your GPU/CPU.

Why learn:

It's the difference that explains privacy and cost.

Key concepts:

Inference, data center, offline.

What it is:

A weights file + an inference program that generates text.

Why learn:

Demystifies the “model” and prepares you for the anatomy (1.4).

Key concepts:

Weights, inference, tokens.

What it is:

Prompts and responses aren't sent to third-party servers.

Why learn:

Critical for sensitive data.

Key concepts:

Sensitive data, no external logs.

What it is:

No per-token cost, enabling continuous use and batch processing.

Why learn:

Cost-effective foundation for 24/7 agents.

Key concepts:

No rate limits, low marginal cost.

What it is:

Independence from price, policy, and region.

Why learn:

Resilience is part of the value.

Key concepts:

Resilience, personal responsibility.

What it is:

Cases where cloud or hybrid makes more sense.

Why learn:

Decide based on criteria, not dogma.

Key concepts:

Hybrid, peak intelligence, cost-effective.

🧬 Anatomy of a local LLM

Weights, tokens, context, quantization, memory, and licenses — no code.

What it is:

Billions of parameters; more ≈ more capable and heavier.

Why learn:

It's the first number you look at when choosing a model.

Key concepts:

Parameters, scale, disk size.

What it is:

Token = piece of a word; context = how much fits in “working memory.”

Why learn:

Long context uses memory—it affects what fits.

Key concepts:

Token, context, KV cache.

What it is:

Reduce the precision of the weights (FP16→Q4) to fit in less memory.

Why learn:

It's what makes affordable hardware possible.

Key concepts:

Accuracy, the quality × size trade-off.

What it is:

Estimate VRAM/RAM by size × quantization.

Why learn:

Connects directly to the hardware choice (Track 2).

Key concepts:

VRAM, RAM, context overhead.

What it is:

The main open model families and what each range is for.

Why learn:

Know where to start when choosing.

Key concepts:

Llama, Qwen, Gemma, Mistral, DeepSeek, Phi.

What it is:

Difference between Apache/MIT and licenses with usage restrictions.

Why learn:

Avoids problems in commercial use.

Key concepts:

License, commercial use, checklist.
